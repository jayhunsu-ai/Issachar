# Evidence Rules

## Rule 1 — Evidence Before Inference

Do not state a business problem as fact unless it is directly supported by evidence.

## Rule 2 — Cite the Source

Every material observation should have a source that can be revisited.

## Rule 3 — Separate Fact From Interpretation

Use explicit fields or language for:

- Observation
- Evidence
- Inference
- Hypothesis
- Recommendation

## Rule 4 — No Manufactured Pain

A missing feature is not automatically a business problem.

A manual-looking process is not proof that automation is desired.

These can be investigation leads, not conclusions.

## Rule 5 — Record Uncertainty

When evidence is incomplete, say what is unknown and what would validate the hypothesis.

## Rule 6 — Prefer Business Impact

Where possible, connect technical observations to potential operational consequences such as lost leads, manual workload, delayed service, duplicated data entry, poor customer self-service, disconnected systems, deployment risk, reporting friction, or operational visibility gaps.

These remain hypotheses until validated.

## Rule 7 — Reproducibility

Another researcher should be able to revisit the evidence and understand how the conclusion was reached.
